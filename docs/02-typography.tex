\ripsection{\ripmdhead{1 · One family}}

\textbf{JetBrains Mono.} Weights \textbf{400}, \textbf{700}, \textbf{800}. That is the entire type system.

\end{ripmdgrid}

\ripcodeopen
\ripcodehead{css}
\ripcodesize{9.0}{13.95}
\begin{ripcodeblock}
--font-mono: "JetBrains Mono", "IBM Plex Mono", ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular,
             Menlo, Consolas, "Liberation Mono", monospace;
\end{ripcodeblock}
\ripcodesizereset\ripcodeclose

\textbf{There is no second family.} Not for headings, not for prose, not for "a bit of contrast". Hierarchy comes from four levers — size, weight, case, tracking — and those four are enough. A humanist sans dropped into a Riposte layout to "warm up the body copy" destroys the premise: this is a document produced by a machine shop.

\textbf{Why a mono for prose?} Because the brand is a spec sheet. The even rhythm of a monospace is the texture of instrument faceplates, terminal output and dimensioned drawings. It reads slightly slower than a proportional face, which is why the measure caps at 66ch and the body sits at a generous 1.55 line-height — the system compensates rather than compromising.


\ripsubsection{\ripmdhead{When the real font is unavailable}}

Upload it. Canva Pro, Figma, Adobe Fonts (as a custom font), Google Workspace via add-on, and every OS font book all accept an OFL font. The licence explicitly permits it.

Only if you genuinely cannot:

\begin{ripmdtable}{X[0.60,l]X[0.62,l]X[1.77,l]}
\ripmdth{TOOL} & \ripmdth{FALLBACK} & \ripmdth{NOTES} \\
Canva (free tier) & \textbf{Space Mono} & Closest in flavour; heavier quirk. Then Roboto Mono. \\
Google Docs/Slides & \textbf{Roboto Mono} & Available natively; the safest cross-platform fallback \\
Word / PowerPoint & \textbf{Consolas} & Ships with Office on every platform \\
Anywhere & \textbf{IBM Plex Mono} & The best overall substitute if installable \\
\end{ripmdtable}

Record it when you do. A deck set in Roboto Mono is off-brand-but-legible; a deck set in Arial is neither.


\ripsection{\ripmdhead{2 · The two registers}}

One voice, two registers. Everything on a Riposte surface is one of these.


\ripsubsection{\ripmdhead{The document — UPPERCASE, tracked}}

Chrome and labels. Filed like a spec sheet.

\ripcodeopen
\ripcodesize{9.0}{13.95}
\begin{ripcodeblock}
DOC NO. RL-200-A      SEC.03      REV. A      SPEC / HEX UNIT      EST. 2026
\end{ripcodeblock}
\ripcodesizereset\ripcodeclose

Uppercase, \ripmono{700} or \ripmono{800}, positive tracking that widens as the size drops. This register never carries a sentence — it carries an identifier.


\ripsubsection{\ripmdhead{The builder — sentence case, 400/700}}

Prose. Plainspoken, concrete, quietly witty. Numbers that matter are bold.

\begin{ripnote}
A disposable vape is, functionally, a lithium cell wrapped in plastic and marketed as trash. Each holds a \textbf{14.46 Wh} cell measuring \textbf{45 × 13 mm}.
\end{ripnote}

Section heads carry a lowercase slash-tag comment, right-aligned: \ripmono{/\allowbreak{}/\allowbreak{} the counter-\allowbreak{}attack}, \ripmono{/\allowbreak{}/\allowbreak{} local loop injection molding}. This is the one place the two registers touch — an uppercase heading with a lowercase code comment trailing it.

Every heading step is a \ripmono{clamp()}. \textbf{Do not add font-size media queries} — the type already scales continuously between the min and max. If a heading is wrong at some viewport, adjust the \ripmono{vw} coefficient, not the breakpoint.


\ripsection{\ripmdhead{4 · The tracking ladder}}

The governing principle: \textbf{tracking widens as type gets smaller and more "machine."} Big statements tighten; micro-labels spread out until they read as engraving.

\ripcodeopen
\ripcodesize{9.0}{13.95}
\begin{ripcodeblock}
-.01em   display        big statements pull together
 .03em   h1 / h2 / h3   uppercase headings need a hair of air
 .08em   nav            links, buttons
 .10em   label          chips, doc numbers
 .14em   tag            // slash tags, corner marks
 .16em   spec           spec-card headers, stamps
 .30em   kicker         pagehead kickers
 .55em   wordmark       L A B O R A T O R I E S   I N C .
\end{ripcodeblock}
\ripcodesizereset\ripcodeclose

Uppercase monospace at 10–12px is genuinely hard to read without tracking — the letterforms are already even-width, so without extra space they fuse into a bar. The ladder is functional, not decorative.

\textbf{Never track lowercase prose.} Tracking applies only to the uppercase register. Body copy sits at \ripmono{0}.


\ripsection{\ripmdhead{5 · Rules of use}}

\begin{ripnumbers}
\item \textbf{Headings and labels are UPPERCASE. Prose is sentence case.} No title case anywhere in the system. Ever.
\item \textbf{Bold the numbers that matter.} In prose, load-bearing figures and units go \ripmono{700}: \textbf{14.46 Wh}, \textbf{45 × 13 mm}, \textbf{72\%}. This is the builder register's one flourish.
\item \textbf{Body copy maxes at 66ch.} Leads pull tighter, to 56–62ch.
\item \textbf{Leads are bold.} \ripmono{--text-lead} is \ripmono{700} by default. A lead paragraph set at 400 reads as body copy that happens to be bigger.
\item \textbf{\ripmono{800} is reserved.} Deck headlines and \ripmono{.stat} numbers only. If everything is 800, nothing is.
\item \textbf{No italics.} Monospace italics are weak and the system has no use for them. For emphasis inside prose use \ripmono{700}, or \ripmono{.hi} (which is \ripmono{700} + \ripmono{--accent-text}).
\item \textbf{No underlines except on links.} And links get \ripmono{2px} thickness at \ripmono{3px} offset — a hairline underline disappears against the mono texture.
\item \textbf{Line-height is 1.55 for prose, 1.15 for headings, 1.02 for display.} Tight display leading is what makes a two-line hero statement read as a single block.
\end{ripnumbers}


\ripsection{\ripmdhead{6 · The wordmark lockup}}

The letterspaced sub-line beneath the mark:

\ripcodeopen
\ripcodesize{9.0}{13.95}
\begin{ripcodeblock}
L A B O R A T O R I E S   I N C .
\end{ripcodeblock}
\ripcodesizereset\ripcodeclose

\begin{ripbullets}
\item Tracking \ripmono{.55em} (\ripmono{-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}tracking-\allowbreak{}wordmark})
\item Uppercase, weight \ripmono{400} — \textbf{not} bold; the mark above carries the weight
\item Optically centred under the wordmark. Because \ripmono{.55em} adds trailing space after the final character, subtract roughly half the tracking value from the right side or it will sit visibly left-of-centre.
\item Size: about 1/8 the height of the wordmark's cap height
\end{ripbullets}

Full logo rules: \ripdocref{\ripmono{04-logo.md}}{RL-BRAND-001-LOGO}.


\ripsection{\ripmdhead{7 · Glyphs, not icons}}

The system has \textbf{no icon font and no icon set.} Meaning is carried by characters that JetBrains Mono already contains:

\ripcodeopen
\ripcodesize{9.0}{13.95}
\begin{ripcodeblock}
▸  list bullets (accent-coloured)
✓  pass / yes            ✕  fail / no            !  warning
→  flow, forward         ↓  flow, stacked        ▾  disclosure caret
◆  diamond marker        (R)  the loop             (@)  contact
\end{ripcodeblock}
\ripcodesizereset\ripcodeclose

No emoji beyond \ripmono{\riploop{}} and \ripmono{\ripmail{}}, which are established site glyphs and render monochrome in this context. Everything else that looks like an icon is a \textbf{hand-built SVG line diagram} in brand colours — see \ripdocref{\ripmono{05-\allowbreak{}patterns.\allowbreak{}md}}{RL-BRAND-001-PAT}.


\ripsection{\ripmdhead{8 · Numbers, units and placeholders}}

\begin{ripbullets}
\item Units get a space: \ripmono{14.46 Wh}, \ripmono{45 × 13 mm}, \ripmono{230 V}. Use \ripmono{×} (U+00D7), not \ripmono{x}.
\item Ranges use an en dash with no spaces: \ripmono{56–62ch}, \ripmono{400–800}.
\item Document control: \ripmono{DOC NO.\allowbreak{} RL-\allowbreak{}000-\allowbreak{}A}, \ripmono{REV. A}, \ripmono{SEC.03}, \ripmono{EST. 2026}.
\item The ticker: \ripmono{PARRY · RIPOSTE · RECYCLE · REPEAT \riploop{}}
\item \textbf{Placeholders are honest and loud.} \ripmono{\$[TAM]}, \ripmono{[Q\_/\_\_]}, flagged \ripmono{⚠ Illustrative — replace before sending}. A placeholder that looks like real data is a liability; make it impossible to ship by accident.
\end{ripbullets}
